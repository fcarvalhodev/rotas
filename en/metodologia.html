Routes · editorial criteria

How we read sources

Facts, accusations and interpretations need different kinds of evidence. We use the same standard for different people and political positions.

What each label means

  • Documented: there is a record of the event or statement. A document containing an accusation proves the accusation exists, not necessarily that it is true.
  • True: the sources support the claim for the period and scope stated.
  • Half true or limited comparison: part of it is supported, but context, comparable measures or scope are missing for the conclusion.
  • False: the claim contradicts identifiable evidence. Absence of proof, on its own, is not enough.
  • Not shown or no proof: the sources consulted do not allow confirmation. That does not prove the event never happened.
  • Open: there is an inquiry or dispute with no conclusion. Investigation, indictment, charges and conviction are distinct stages.

Comparable numbers

We state the period, unit, scope and method. A stock is not a sum of projects; bank transactions are not income; a forecast is not a disbursement; MW measures power and MWh measures energy. Historical series must use the same version and population. A correlation or a difference between governments does not by itself show causation.

Sources and dates

We prefer laws, rulings, statistical databases and original documents. News reports and statements by the parties remain identified as such when no full document is accessible. We do not certify sealed case files based on leaks. The page's general date marks the editorial review; charts and cases keep their own reference dates.

Classifications of sovereignty, influence or political position are editorial interpretations, with criteria explained on the page. The selection of episodes is not an exhaustive inventory.
